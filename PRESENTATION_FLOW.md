# PRESENTATION_FLOW.md

## Mainline Flow Overview

The mainline presentation should feel like a guided spatial story of Jeju: first orient the audience, then explain the island's structure, then show signature places, then translate that into routes, and finally close with practical travel judgment.

## Page 1: Cover / Jeju Overview

Core point:
Jeju is best understood as an explorable island world rather than a list of attractions.

Suggested visuals:

- Full 16:9 island overview
- Hallasan at center
- East coast with Seongsan silhouette
- Udo off the coast
- Coastal roads, villages, boats, orchards, cliffs, waterfalls
- Small animated-feeling life details such as cars, walkers, markets, and sea movement

Speaker notes:

- Open by framing Jeju as a compact island with unusually high landscape variety.
- Explain that this presentation is navigated like a map, not consumed like a linear slide deck.
- Invite the audience to read the island visually before drilling into details.

## Page 2: Spatial Structure

Core point:
Jeju's geography explains its travel logic: central mountain, ring-like roads, coastal clusters, and east-west differences.

Suggested visuals:

- Simplified island massing
- Hallasan as geographic anchor
- Directional region zoning
- Ring-road logic
- Clear separation of mountain interior, coast, town clusters, and satellite island

Speaker notes:

- Explain how the center-periphery structure shapes travel time and itinerary planning.
- Contrast scenic interior movement with coastal circulation.
- Prepare the audience to understand why attractions group naturally by geography.

## Page 3: Core Sight Map

Core point:
Jeju's signature experiences are distributed across landscape, coast, geology, food, and cultural zones.

Suggested visuals:

- Highlighted landmark clusters
- Seongsan Ilchulbong, Udo, Jusangjeolli, waterfalls, Hallasan
- Culture and food zones
- Market node in Jeju City

Speaker notes:

- Show that Jeju is not one highlight but a network of experiences.
- Use hotspots to branch into two or three key examples depending on audience interest.
- Reinforce that landmarks are part of a broader ecosystem of movement, food, and local identity.

## Page 4: Recommended Routes

Core point:
The best Jeju experience comes from route design, not checklist tourism.

Suggested visuals:

- One-day route overlay
- Three-day route overlay
- East-focused scenic loop
- Balanced island sampler loop
- Visual differentiation between driving, ferry transfer, market stop, and walking zone

Speaker notes:

- Translate spatial knowledge into practical travel sequences.
- Explain tradeoffs between short stay efficiency and deeper island coverage.
- Use route pages to show how geography becomes planning intelligence.

## Page 5: Travel Advice Summary

Core point:
Good Jeju travel depends on season, pacing, weather awareness, and expectation setting.

Suggested visuals:

- Seasonal mini-world or segmented atlas panel
- Weather, wind, bloom, tangerine, peak hiking, and crowd cues
- Practical travel iconography kept outside the art core

Speaker notes:

- Close by converting scenic inspiration into usable advice.
- Summarize when to go, how to move, and where to spend more time.
- End with the message that Jeju rewards curated pacing over over-scheduling.

## Extension Pages

### Hallasan

Core point:
Hallasan is the island's geographic and emotional center.

Suggested visuals:

- Close-up mountain world
- Trails, ridges, crater cues, alpine vegetation, weather shift

Speaker notes:

- Emphasize elevation, symbolic centrality, and time commitment.

### Seongsan Ilchulbong

Core point:
Seongsan is a coastal volcanic icon with strong sunrise identity.

Suggested visuals:

- Tuff cone by sea, stair path, sunrise atmosphere, nearby fields

Speaker notes:

- Frame it as one of Jeju's most recognizable forms and a strong eastern anchor.

### Udo

Core point:
Udo offers a lighter, slower satellite-island rhythm.

Suggested visuals:

- Ring-road coastline, bikes, beaches, ferry link, open fields

Speaker notes:

- Position it as a pace shift rather than just another attraction.

### Jusangjeolli Cliffs

Core point:
Jeju's geology becomes visible and architectural at the coast.

Suggested visuals:

- Basalt column cliffs with surf impact

Speaker notes:

- Explain the volcanic identity through form and texture.

### Waterfalls

Core point:
Water features reveal Jeju's subtropical side and scenic variety.

Suggested visuals:

- Dramatic falls, lush vegetation, rock walls, mist

Speaker notes:

- Contrast waterfalls with the harsher volcanic coast to show range.

### Jeju Culture

Core point:
Jeju is not only scenic; it has a distinct symbolic and lived culture.

Suggested visuals:

- Dol hareubang, stone walls, haenyeo references, village textures

Speaker notes:

- Keep this page grounded in symbols, everyday environment, and island identity.

### Food

Core point:
Food is a major part of Jeju's memory and place experience.

Suggested visuals:

- Black pork grills, seafood, citrus, warm evening dining clusters

Speaker notes:

- Present food as atmosphere plus regional specialty, not a menu list.

### Dongmun Market

Core point:
Dongmun Market condenses local energy into a dense urban food node.

Suggested visuals:

- Stalls, roof rhythms, lights, shoppers, packaged local produce

Speaker notes:

- Use it as the strongest urban contrast to the island's natural scenes.

### One-day Route

Core point:
A short Jeju visit requires disciplined geographic selection.

Suggested visuals:

- Tight route overlay, timing logic, one flagship east or mixed route

Speaker notes:

- Clarify what must be omitted to keep the day enjoyable.

### Three-day Route

Core point:
Three days is enough to balance scenery, food, and pacing.

Suggested visuals:

- Three-part route composition with day-based logic

Speaker notes:

- Show how to layer east, central, and urban/coastal experiences.

### Best Seasons

Core point:
Season dramatically changes Jeju's mood and ideal activities.

Suggested visuals:

- Seasonal panels with bloom, greenery, wind, clear skies, tangerine cues

Speaker notes:

- Help the audience match expectations to season rather than asking for one universal best time.

### Travel Tips

Core point:
Comfort and logistics shape the trip as much as scenery does.

Suggested visuals:

- Advice layer over simplified map fragments or travel vignettes

Speaker notes:

- Cover car use, weather variability, walking assumptions, booking timing, and pacing.
